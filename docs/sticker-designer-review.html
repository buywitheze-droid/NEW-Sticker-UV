What a customer can do today

  1. Upload raster artwork or a PDF/SVG rendered for editing. PDF import may detect an existing named cut path; other imports use generated-cut options.
  2. Set width/height in inches with aspect lock and presets, edit the image, choose Contour or Shape, configure fills/spot colors, inspect a canvas preview and download a single-design PDF. Authenticated users can save a design through serializeDesign.
  3. Add the current design as an in-memory gang-sheet item. It begins at quantity 1. “Fill Sheet” increases copies of the last-added design. The panel offers quantity controls, size presets, item PDFs, gang-sheet PDFs and authenticated “Save & Download.”
  4. These are design/prepress and downloadable-asset actions, not a submitted sticker order. The inspected page/panel has no material SKU, die-cut/kiss-cut purchase selection, price, shipping, cart, payment or order confirmation.

Actual shape versus contour and imported-cut paths

A selected shape describes the cut boundary, not a guarantee that the underlying artwork has that silhouette. The relevant paths are materially different:

  • Generated Contour. strokeSettings.enabled requests an image/alpha-derived trace through processContourInWorker. Shape detection, background/alpha and trace settings affect the outcome. Cached data includes cut and preview paths, dimensions and artwork offsets. downloadContourPDF emits a vector spot-color cut path. Reprocessing before single export and add-to-sheet is intended to avoid a stale debounced cache; tracing quality still depends on the source and algorithm.
  • Generated Shape. shapeSettings.enabled is mutually exclusive with generated Contour in the controls. Circle, oval, square, rectangle and rounded variants produce geometry around/over the design. calculateShapeDimensions combines image size with shape offset; overrides, corner radius, rotation, image positioning, fill and bleed influence output. downloadShapePDF writes a generated vector cut. Circle selects a circular trim and clipped/filled print image; it does not turn arbitrary source pixels into circular artwork.
  • Imported cut PDF. If a PDF cut path is found, handlePDFUpload does not trim the page because extracted paths use untrimmed page coordinates. Controls show the detected label and suppress ordinary generated-shape settings. handleDownload routes to generatePDFWithVectorCutContour before the normal branches. It places a rasterized image and extracted page-coordinate paths into a new PDF with a vector Separation cut path; it does not guarantee byte-for-byte retention of original PDF content. Relabeling is not a geometry edit.
  • Locked contour. Optional lockedContour is stored with saved designs and can be drawn as an additional labeled path in generated contour/shape exports. It is distinct from the PDF-imported cut and the active Shape choice. Its badge alone does not establish that a gang-sheet item retains it.
  • No cut. Single-design export asks for confirmation before design-only output. On the sheet, noCutPath can carry a rectangle for packing without emitting a cut line. That rectangle is a footprint, not a die line.

Units, size and bleed: three different boundaries

  • Editor dimensions, contour margin, shape offset/radius and overrides, sheet size, gap and padding are modeled in inches; PDF geometry is drawn in 72 points per inch. Raster operations use effective DPI or a 300-DPI shape clipping canvas. Extracted PDF paths start in page pixel coordinates and convert using page size/DPI.
  • Generated contour code includes a 0.10-inch page margin around traced bounds (bleedInches). generateShapePathPointsInches adds 0.10 inch on each side of shape dimensions; shape PDF output has an outer page allowance. Page allowance is not automatically printed color bleed. Shape bleedEnabled/bleedColor control separate artwork treatment. White or transparent space outside the cut is not extended ink.
  • Shape size, image size and packed footprint are different. The packer places contourData.widthInches/heightInches (including allowance), though the visual cut can sit inside that box. Sheet defaults: 48 × 12 inches; presets extend to 48 × 60; edge padding defaults to 0.25 inch and minimum contour gap is 0.25 inch.

Product implication: label final trim, artwork extent and outer packing footprint separately. Verify cut-to-ink coverage per export mode before claiming a universal bleed guarantee or quoting a size to a buyer.

Designer preview is not an approved production proof

PreviewSection paints artwork, cutline/spot state and sheet placements on a canvas. PDF export follows separate raster and spot-vector code and may use a higher-resolution source. A persuasive on-screen preview does not establish correct bleed, named Separation colors, closed paths, clipping, DPI or every placed copy in the actual exported file.

A real proof should be rendered from the same finalized PDF asset that production receives. It should expose cut and bleed overlays, measured trim dimensions, material/cut semantics, preflight issues and a recorded customer approval of an immutable revision. Clicking “Download” or “Save & Download” is not approval. Screen color is indicative, not a calibrated color guarantee.

Sticker ordering, quantity, overflow & persistence risks

  • Quantity is currently sheet copies, not a commerce quantity. packGangSheet returns placements and overflow from rectangular item bounds. Rotation can occur; interlocking silhouette nesting is not used. expandSettingsToFit grows sheet height through presets where possible. Fill Sheet targets the last item.
  • Incomplete sheets can remain exportable. Quantity entry can be clamped with a brief warning if the maximum sheet is reached. Selecting a smaller preset can create overflow. The panel warns, but export/save are disabled only if placements are empty—not whenever overflow exists. PDF generation receives placements, while Save & Download records totalQuantity from requested item quantities. Requested, placed, persisted and later billed counts could disagree unless a future order handoff blocks unresolved overflow.
  • Imported cuts on gang sheets need verification. Add-to-sheet asks for worker contour data even for a PDF with an existing path, snapshots cached generated geometry when present, otherwise creates a rectangular fallback. GangSheetItem does not contain imported PDF cutContourPoints or lockedContour. The single-PDF preservation branch therefore cannot be assumed to carry over to sheet output. Inspect actual exported PDF paths/separations before promising this workflow.
  • A saved design is not an approved press file. Design serialization keeps source-asset reference, settings, locked contour and spot tags. Saving a gang sheet uploads its generated PDF with dimensions, item count and total quantity; editor gangSheetItems remain component state rather than a reloadable itemized cart. Reopening/editing a design does not restore an approved order revision.
  • Spot-name collision checks between White, Gloss and CutContour help, but a unique name alone cannot certify geometry or printability.

The strongest differentiator is geometry the buyer can trust: consistent measurements through designer, sheet, proof and order; visible cut-versus-print distinction; traceable approval; and a clear statement of what will arrive. Do not advertise unsupported materials or processes.

Ranked improvements & acceptance criteria

Everything below is a recommendation. None of these end-to-end product features is asserted to be implemented.

P0 Production correctness & truthful handoff

  1. Make cut-source selection explicit and consistent. Benefit: sheet and single PDF cannot silently use different die lines. Accept when: each item has a normalized inch-based cut source/geometry; imported multi-path cuts and intentional locked secondary paths are preserved across preview, single and sheet export or explicitly rejected; PDF checks compare path count, bounds, labels and closure after rotation. Never present a rectangular fallback as a cut.
  2. Fail closed on overflow and quantity disagreement. Benefit: no saved/paid job drops copies. Accept when: export, save and order block for overflow or unplaceable items; affected designs and requested/placed/remaining counts are named; add-sheet or reduce-quantity resolves the issue; PDF count matches persisted totals.
  3. Preflight the exported artifact and require approval. Benefit: production receives what the buyer approved. Accept when: required named separations, finite closed paths, page/trim size, alignment and printable resolution are checked; failures block order, warnings require acknowledgement, and proof/order reference one versioned PDF asset/hash.
  4. Validate bleed and size per mode. Benefit: fewer halos, clips and incorrect sizes. Accept when: UI separates trim, art and footprint; measured cut-to-print coverage warns on each edge; shape, contour, imported and gang-sheet PDFs meet agreed size tolerances.

P1 Usable ordering after correctness

  1. Constrained product configurator and checkout handoff. Benefit: a real order path. Accept when: combinations come from a supported catalog; price/fulfillment terms appear before approval; server validates selections and stores them with the approved asset; confirmation shows order ID and line items.
  2. Recoverable itemized job drafts. Benefit: multi-design work survives a return visit. Accept when: authenticated restore includes item sources, settings, quantities, cut-source/version, spot choices and sheet settings; missing assets are explicit errors; any edit invalidates approval and reruns packing/preflight.
  3. File-based production proof UI. Benefit: buyers see the actual deliverable. Accept when: a finalized-PDF render offers art/trim/bleed/backing views, dimensions and placed counts with explicit revision approval; the screen mockup and color limitations are distinguished.

P2 Differentiation & polish

  1. Guided contour repair. Benefit: fix difficult art without hiding risk. Accept when: tiny islands, holes, thin bridges, excessive nodes and ambiguous backgrounds identify affected regions and reversible choices; inspected PDF geometry matches the selection.
  2. Honest sheet-efficiency guidance. Benefit: informed size/quantity decisions. Accept when: supported sheet sizes, required sheets, area utilization, remaining copies and rotation are shown while enforcing clearance; no claim of silhouette nesting.
  3. Traceability and accessible controls. Benefit: easier support and repeat orders. Accept when: revision history links decisions, hash, preflight and approval to an order; keyboard and screen-reader users can select cut source, inspect issues and approve without canvas/color-only cues.
